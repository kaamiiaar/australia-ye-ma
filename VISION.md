# Australia-ye Ma — Product Vision

## The idea

Australia-ye Ma (استرالیای ما, “Our Australia”) is the trusted local layer for Persian-speaking life across Australia: one place to discover businesses, events, activities, jobs, communities, useful guides, translators, and second-hand goods. Melbourne-e Ma (ملبورنِ ما) is its first city experience and launch identity.

Today this information is scattered across Telegram channels, Instagram pages, university groups, websites, and word of mouth. Search is difficult, old posts remain visible, trust is unclear, and newcomers often do not know where to begin. Australia-ye Ma turns this fragmented information into a searchable, current, location-aware product.

## Who it is for

- Persian-speaking newcomers who need practical help settling in Australia.
- Established Iranian and Persian-speaking residents looking for local services, events, communities, and opportunities.
- Students seeking university groups, work, housing essentials, and social connections.
- Australian businesses and professionals that serve Persian-speaking customers.
- Community organisers who need a better way to reach the right local audience.

The initial market is Melbourne. The model should later expand city by city across Australia.

## Core promise

**Find what you need, near you, in a language you trust.**

The app should make a useful local answer reachable in under a minute. It should feel welcoming and community-led without becoming an unsearchable social feed.

## Why Australia-ye Ma alongside Telegram

Telegram is where community conversation happens. Australia-ye Ma is where that fragmented knowledge becomes searchable, current, and useful.

The product brings together cities and categories with structured search, maps, comparisons, saved items, and alerts. It removes expired or duplicate listings, separates verified guidance from community opinion, personalises discovery, and still routes people to the relevant Telegram communities when conversation is the better experience.

## Product pillars

1. **Discover** — businesses, services, events, activities, guides, and places.
2. **Connect** — sport, university, professional, newcomer, family, and social communities.
3. **Opportunity** — jobs, sponsorship-aware roles, translators, volunteering, and collaboration.
4. **Marketplace** — safe, local second-hand buying and selling.
5. **Trust** — transparent sources, verification, reporting, freshness, and community moderation.

## First product wedge

The first version should not attempt to launch every marketplace at once. It should become genuinely useful in Melbourne through:

- a high-quality Persian-friendly business directory;
- practical, reviewed newcomer and student knowledge;
- community discovery with links back to active Telegram groups;
- lightweight submissions from users and organisers;
- visible verification and last-updated information.

Fresh jobs, rentals, events, and second-hand listings can appear as prototype categories, but should not become a core operational promise until users, businesses, and organisers supply them directly. During bootstrap, a small editorial check of major public sources may occasionally identify useful leads; it is not a comprehensive or real-time feed.

## Trust and listing policy

Listings may be created from public business information when collection and reuse are permitted, but they must never imply consent, partnership, endorsement, or verification.

Every imported listing should include:

- `Unverified` status by default;
- the public source and date last checked;
- a clear `Claim this listing` action;
- an easy correction and removal process;
- no sensitive or personal information that is not necessary;
- no user reviews until identity, moderation, and dispute processes exist.

Verified status should require the owner or organiser to prove control of a business domain, phone number, social account, or other reliable channel. Sponsored placement must be visibly labelled and must not silently affect organic ranking.

Do not scrape private or closed communities. Do not build the product around mass or continuous scraping of Telegram channels. Telegram and third-party directories such as `auiran.com` may help discover topics, communities, or public business leads, but published facts should be independently checked against an authoritative or owner-controlled web source. Do not copy community messages or third-party editorial text. Prefer organiser submissions, permitted APIs/feeds, public business websites, and carefully reviewed manual imports. Users should explicitly consent before content submitted through a Telegram bot is retained and published.

## Experience principles

- Persian-first and fully usable in English; support right-to-left and left-to-right layouts from the start.
- Location-aware, but useful without location permission.
- Search and filters before feeds and engagement mechanics.
- Show freshness, source, and verification wherever trust matters.
- Let people browse before requiring an account.
- Use plain language and avoid migration or sponsorship promises the platform cannot verify.
- Keep community safety more important than growth metrics.

## What success looks like

For the Melbourne pilot:

- users repeatedly find a relevant result and contact or save it;
- verified businesses keep their details current;
- organisers submit events directly instead of relying only on reposts;
- useful listings remain current through expiry and re-verification;
- weekly returning users grow through word of mouth, Telegram, and Instagram;
- supply quality and successful connections matter more than raw listing count.

North-star metric: **weekly successful local connections** — a meaningful contact, event registration, saved opportunity, community join, or marketplace enquiry originating in Australia-ye Ma.

## Business model direction

Keep basic discovery and community participation free. Potential revenue later:

- clearly labelled promoted listings;
- business profiles with enhanced tools and analytics;
- paid event promotion;
- recruiter or employer tools;
- carefully selected local partnerships.

Never sell verification, hide basic correction tools, or allow payment to erase negative safety signals.

## Long-term product shape

- Mobile-first web app and Telegram Mini App for rapid iteration.
- Native iOS app as the primary polished consumer experience once the core loops are proven.
- Android app alongside iOS when usage justifies native distribution, ideally from a shared cross-platform codebase.
- A small web admin console for moderation, imports, verification, and content freshness.

## September 2026 prototype extension

The approved prototype now includes Australian news and events, source-labelled compact story cards and an optional photo/video browsing view. General sports and unrelated world news are outside the editorial scope. Exchange screens are informational prototypes with clearly labelled sample rates and a strict toman/IRR distinction; they do not transfer money. These additions do not introduce a backend or a claim of live/comprehensive coverage.
